The leverage from AI in sales is simple to state: let it carry the repeatable work, so the team spends its hours where a human wins, in the relationship and the judgment. I build toward that on purpose, and I lead the team through the change that comes with it.

## Why it matters

A seller's edge has always been trust and judgment. The admin around selling, the note-taking, the CRM hygiene, the pattern-spotting across calls, is exactly the work a model does well. Hand that to the machine and you give every rep back the hours and the attention that win deals, which is the whole point.

<Note>
  AI drafts and accelerates. The human owns the judgment and the relationship.
</Note>

## How I build it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Point agents at the admin, people at the relationship">
    An agent fills the CRM: the meeting notes, the next steps and their dates, the qualification markers. The rep spends that reclaimed time with the customer. The system of record stays current on its own, and the rep's evening is their own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the thin differentiated layer, buy the commodity underneath">
    I have built sales tooling on an AI model with no engineering support, including a call-intelligence and coaching tool that reviews every call and drafts methodology-specific feedback. The lesson I carry from it: build the part that is specific to your team, your coaching methodology, your judgment, and buy the plumbing underneath, the ingestion and the dashboards, from someone who runs it at scale. That is where the leverage is, and where the upkeep stays sane.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coach with the signal the tool surfaces">
    Call intelligence turns a stack of recordings into the specific coaching moment for each rep, so every rep gets feedback on every call. A person reviews every output before it reaches a rep, because the tool informs the coaching and the conversation stays human.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lead the team through the change">
    The most valuable part is bringing the reps along. I frame AI as the thing that makes a seller look better and earn more, because it clears the busywork and sharpens the deal, so the team pulls it toward themselves. Setting that tone is a leadership job, and it is what makes a team adopt.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Agents I build

These are real agents I have built and run. Each one takes a slow, manual part of selling and does it at scale, with a person reviewing the output.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Call coach" icon="headset">
    Scores a discovery call from the transcript on the selling framework and writes a constructive, evidence-grounded coaching recap.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Deal and pipeline assessor" icon="magnifying-glass-chart">
    Grades every opportunity on MEDDPICC from real customer evidence, audits the inbox for owed follow-ups, and recommends the next action.
  </Card>
  <Card title="CRM scribe" icon="pen-to-square">
    Writes the call notes, next steps and dates, and qualification markers straight into the CRM.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Prospect researcher" icon="user-magnifying-glass">
    Mines public signals into ranked, evidence-backed business problems and a research-first brief for the account.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Pipeline-generation workbench" icon="list-check">
    Scores and prioritizes accounts, verifies contacts, and drafts personalized outreach for the week.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Outreach packager" icon="paper-plane">

  </Card>

## What good looks like

- The CRM stays current on its own, because the agent keeps it that way.
- Every rep is coached on every call.
- Reps reach for the tools themselves, because the tools make them money.
- The team's hours concentrate on the relationship and the judgment, where the human still wins.
