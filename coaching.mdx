Every rep needs something different from me, and what they need changes as they grow. I match where I sit on the trainer-to-coach spectrum to each rep's stage, coach from evidence so the feedback is objective and trusted, and close every session on a clear commitment. I run the same discipline one level up with my managers, so the whole organization coaches to one standard.

## Why it matters

Coaching is how the playbook transfers from the leader to the team and how the floor rises. It is the highest-return hour a leader spends per rep, because a good coaching conversation shows up in every call that rep runs afterward. Done consistently, it turns one strong seller's instincts into the whole team's default.

<Note>
  Meet each manager or rep where they are. Train the ones learning the motion, coach the ones who have it, and ask them which they need.
</Note>

## The trainer-coach spectrum

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Train" icon="dumbbell">
    For a rep new or ramping. Run the calls alongside them and show the motion until it is theirs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Coach" icon="lightbulb">
    For a rep who has the motion. Ask the questions that let them find the answer themselves.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Where I sit slides with the rep's stage, from new and ramping, to developing, to tenured. I ask each rep which they need, and I run the same spectrum one level up with my managers.

## How I do it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Match the spectrum to the individual's stage">
    An early manager or rep needs training: I show them the motion and run calls alongside them. As a rep grows, I shift to coaching, asking the questions that let them find the answer themselves. I also ask each rep directly which one they want this week, because they usually know where they are, and giving them the choice makes the hour theirs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coach from evidence and data">
    I ground every coaching moment in the call transcript, the numbers, and cross-functional input, so the feedback is objective and the rep trusts it. I use an LLM as a neutral judge on the recording to surface the specific moment worth working on, then I coach the person. Evidence makes the conversation about the work, which is where a rep is most open to growing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it stick: close on well, different, and a commitment">
    I end every coaching call the same way: one thing the rep did well, one thing they would do differently, and a single clear commitment for the next week. Naming the win builds the habit, the one change keeps it focused, and the commitment is what carries the coaching into the next real call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coach the coaches">
    I run the exact same spectrum and the same evidence discipline with my managers. I sit in on their one-to-ones and their pipeline-generation sessions, coach how they coach, and hold them to the same well, different, and commitment close with their own reps. Managing managers means raising the coaches, so the floor rises across every team at once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The spectrum, in practice

<Tip>
  One AE was early in ramp and building toward a full pipeline. That called for training: I sat beside them and we made cold calls together, live, practicing the opening and the follow-up until the motion was theirs. Training is doing the work alongside a rep until it sticks.

  A manager on the team was ready for something different. There the work moved one level up: I coached how they ran the pipeline-generation session for their own team, asked the questions that got them to a sharper plan, and held them to it. Coaching is helping someone who already has the motion lead it better. Same leader, two points on the spectrum, each person met where they were.
</Tip>

## What good looks like

- Every rep is coached every week, on a call they chose, and leaves with one commitment.
- Coaching lands, because it is grounded in evidence the rep can see for themselves.
- Managers coach their own reps to the same standard, so the floor rises across every team.
- Reps carry the habit with them, and many still call for advice years after they have moved on.
