description: "A few standing moments every week, each with one job, that make the pipeline, the forecast, coaching, and enablement repeatable."
---

A team runs on its calendar. A few standing moments every week, each with one clear job, are what make everything else repeatable: the pipeline gets built, the forecast is trustworthy, every rep gets coached, and the team keeps sharpening its craft. This is the cadence I install.

## Why it matters

Consistency is a product of rhythm. When the same few moments happen at the same time every week, the team operates on a steady cadence, and a leader can coach the system while the pipeline runs on its rhythm. The rhythm is the container that the pipeline engine and the comp plan both live inside.

<Note>
  Reps own their pipeline. The leader owns the engine that feeds it.
</Note>

## The week

<Steps>
  <Step title="Monday: commit the forecast by gate">
    Every deal is called by the stage it has actually reached, with the solutions engineer on the call, so the week opens on a number built the same way every time. A forecast made the same way each Monday is a forecast the whole team can trust.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tuesday: generate pipeline together">
    The standing session where the team prospects as one, led from the front. It is the most important hour on the calendar, and it sets the tempo for the rest of the week.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Thursday: enablement, alternating sales and product">
    A standing enablement session runs every Thursday, alternating week to week between two tracks. One week builds the fundamentals of selling: discovery, demo, negotiation, the craft that travels with a rep anywhere. The next goes company-specific: the competitors, the markets, the ICPs, and the positioning that win in this market. New hires certify on both tracks before they carry a full quota, and the weekly session keeps the whole team sharp as the market moves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Friday: coach one to one">
    Each rep brings a call they want to work on. We coach that call, set next week's prospecting plan, and agree on three commitments. The week closes on development and loads the spring for the next one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Friday: run the cross-functional alignment call">
    The SDR leads, marketing, partners, and events meet as one group to report the week's numbers, each bringing the top, middle, and bottom-of-funnel results they own, plus one thing that worked and one thing they will do differently. When the leader runs the engine at this table, the reps are free to spend their attention on customers.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Keep the numbers in daylight all week, so Monday morning only confirms what the team already sees.
</Note>

## The thread that ties it together

<Tip>
  I send one weekly recap to the whole team: the numbers that matter, a shout-out for the wins, and the one thing we are focused on next. It keeps progress visible to everyone and makes the wins worth chasing, so accountability lives in the open and the wins are celebrated where everyone can see them. People rise to a standard they can see.
</Tip>

## What good looks like

- The forecast is trusted, because it is built the same way every Monday.
- Every rep is coached every week, on a call they chose to work on.
- The team sharpens its craft on a steady cadence, so skills compound and new hires certify before they carry a quota.
- Marketing, SDRs, and partners pull toward the same accounts, because the engine call aligns them.
- Wins are visible to the whole team, and the number is a known quantity all week long.
