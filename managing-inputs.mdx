Revenue is a lagging number. I run a team on the inputs that produce it, so progress is visible as it happens and a gain can be coached into place before it shows up in the close. Manage the inputs and the output takes care of itself.

## Why it matters

The leading indicators move weeks before revenue does, so they can be coached while there is still time to shape the quarter. Running on inputs makes management a weekly conversation about the handful of behaviors that create the number.

<Note>
  Manage the inputs, and the output takes care of itself.
</Note>

## How I run on inputs

<Steps>
  <Step title="Lead with the indicators that move first">
    Track the behaviors that predict revenue and show up weeks ahead of it. These are the numbers I coach on in the one-to-ones and review on the engine call, because by the time they move, the revenue is already on its way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Measure each rep on the stage they are in">
    A ramping rep is measured on meetings created, a maturing rep on mid-stage pipeline, a tenured rep on closed revenue. Matching the metric to the stage shows exactly where a rep's funnel needs help, and it lets a new hire earn a real win early, on the behavior they can control right now.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the spread between top and median">
    The number I care most about is the distance between the top rep and the median rep. Closing that gap is how a team compounds, because it means the playbook is transferring and the floor is coming up. The aim is simple: the median rep performing like the top one. Managing managers, I run the same lens one level up, and coach each manager to close the spread inside their own team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Put a few of them in daylight">
    Pick the two or three indicators that matter most this quarter and put them on a shared dashboard the whole team sees. Visible inputs become the thing people manage themselves, so accountability runs on its own and the weekly rhythm has something concrete to turn on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The inputs I watch

I run the dashboard at two levels, and I lead with the managers: whether each manager is running the system that makes their reps succeed, then what each rep is doing.

### Manager inputs

| Input | What it tells me |
| --- | --- |
| Call reviews per rep | Whether every rep is coached every week, on every manager's team |
| Operating-rhythm adherence | Whether each manager runs the Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday cadence |
| Team pipeline coverage | Each team carrying enough pipeline to hit its number |
| Forecast accuracy by manager | Each manager's committed number against what lands |
| Top-to-median spread by team | Whether each manager is raising their own floor |

### Rep inputs

| Input | What it tells me |
| --- | --- |
| Meetings created per rep | The clearest early signal of a healthy hunter |
| Demo to evaluation rate | Whether the first conversation earns a real next step |
| Self-sourced pipeline share | How much of the pipeline the team created itself |
| Average days in stage per rep | How quickly deals move through each stage, rep by rep |
| Active deals per rep | Whether each rep is carrying a full, workable load |
| New deals added weekly | The rate the team creates fresh pipeline, every week |

## What good looks like

- Problems surface weeks early, while there is still time to coach them.
- Every rep knows the one or two inputs that move their number, and manages to them.
- The top-to-median gap is narrowing quarter over quarter.
- The forecast is a calm read of the inputs, because the inputs were managed all along.
