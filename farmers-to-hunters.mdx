Most sales orgs that grew up on inbound become farming orgs by default. The reps can sell, and the talent is real. The system rewarded order-taking, so order-taking is what it produced. Here is how I turn that around while protecting a team that is already hitting its number.

## Why it happens

When demand walks in the door, the behavior that wins is responsiveness. That works right up until inbound stops scaling, which it always does, and fastest in enterprise. The tell shows up in a single quarter: attainment stops being uniform across the team, self-sourced pipeline is thin, and the quarter is riding on inbound.

The fix starts with what the team is paid to do. Change the incentives first, and let the structure follow once the behavior has moved.

<Note>
  The character you want is the same. It is the incentive plan that tells a different story.
</Note>

## The sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fix the incentives before you touch the org chart">
    Incentives drive behavior. Pay on the leading indicators of hunting: the net-new meetings a rep creates and the live evaluations they start, measured by clear customer actions. Make the behavior you want the behavior that pays, and build it into the comp plan itself, so the dashboards and the weekly rhythm organize around it on their own.

    Hold one quota-to-OTE multiplier across every rep. I run a 5x. Then put a real accelerator past 100 percent, because as a rep's OTE climbs their quota climbs with it, so the raise keeps moving the finish line. A strong accelerator makes the next deal the fastest path to more pay, and that is how the real hunters show themselves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess the talent, and start with the leaders">
    Put every manager on the hunter-to-farmer spectrum first, because the managers set the ceiling for the reps underneath them. Then assess the reps. Act quickly where you find a will or character gap, and keep the assessment ahead of any structural change, so the team you build on is the one you chose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Install the hunting mechanics, and model them from the front">
    Stand up pipeline generation as a shared, standing weekly motion, and run the first calls yourself. A leader on the phones with real energy sets the standard the team rises to, and earns the right to hold them to it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hire every net-new seat to the hunter profile">
    For each seat you add, hire to the team you want to become. The strongest signal is someone who earned their way up at a single company; the next is a background that required winning over strangers every day. Who you hire reshapes a culture faster than anything else you can do.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Where the biggest multiples come from

The biggest multiples I have seen come from bringing hunting energy inside existing accounts, treating an installed customer as a territory to win. I have taken single accounts up more than tenfold that way. Point the hunters at the base you already have, and expansion becomes the fastest revenue in the building.

## What good looks like

- Self-sourced pipeline is rising as a share of the total, quarter over quarter.
- The gap between your top rep and your median rep is narrowing.
- Attainment is distributed across the roster, with the forecast spread across many deals.
- The whole thing runs on inputs. Manage the inputs and the output takes care of itself, until the median rep performs like the top one.
