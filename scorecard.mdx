[Managing the inputs](/managing-inputs) tells a team what to watch. The scorecard is how I make the team run on it every week. It reads the same funnel at three depths: the weekly board is whether a rep is doing the work, the monthly board is whether that work is progressing, with deals converting and moving through the funnel, and a trailing result view confirms whether it produced, quota and win rate on a rolling window. I write the scoring out in full, so an agent can read this page, pull the CRM, and fill it in with nothing added but the team's own targets and field names. Where those are not wired up yet, the agent works with the user to find them or approximate them, so the score starts now and sharpens over time.

<Note>
  Every line is a counted event. A meeting happened, a customer signed, a buyer logged in, a deal advanced a stage. That keeps the board a fact, and keeps every coaching conversation pointed at one.
</Note>

## Why it matters

Revenue is a lagging number, so I run the team on the inputs that produce it, and the scorecard is where those inputs become something I rank and coach on a Monday. The weekly board holds what a rep controls right now, all of it counted events, so the ranking is fair and the one-to-one starts from a fact instead of an opinion. The monthly board reads whether that work is progressing, deals converting and moving, which is the earliest honest signal of performance when a deal can take two or three quarters to close. The trailing view holds the result, read on a slower clock where the closes have settled. Together they show a rep their week, their trajectory, and their number.

## How I score it

The board runs in one of two modes, and the team picks by whether it wants to set targets. Points mode scores each line against a target and sums to one hundred, so it keeps magnitude and shows how far ahead the top rep is. Ranking mode sets nothing: it ranks the reps against each other on every line and adds up the positions, so it produces a defensible stack-rank with no calibration at all. The weights are the same in both modes, and quota stays absolute in the trailing view either way.

### Points mode

A count line scores its count against a target, the two source-weighted lines value self-sourced work at five times inbound, a conversion line scores its rate against a benchmark, and a lower-is-better line like velocity or stall scores the inverse. Each line's points are its score times its weight, and the composite is the sum.

```
line_pct   = min(1, actual / target)        # count lines
line_pct   = min(1, rate / benchmark)       # conversion lines
line_pct   = min(1, benchmark / actual)     # lower-is-better lines, velocity and stall
effective  = 5 * self_sourced + 1 * inbound # source-weighted lines
line_pts   = line_pct * line_weight
composite  = sum(line_pts)                   # 0 to 100, higher is better
```

If a single line has no target yet, index it to the current top performer for that line so it still scores, and mark it provisional until a target is set.

### Ranking mode

Rank the reps within their segment on each line, best to worst, one is best. Multiply each rep's position by the line's weight, and sum across the lines. The lowest weighted total is the top rep. No targets, no benchmarks, only the reps compared to each other.

```
position   = rank on this line, within segment   # 1 = best
line_score = position * line_weight
total      = sum(line_score)                 # lowest is best
```

Because the weight multiplies the position, a weak spot on a heavier line costs more, so BAMFAM at twenty outweighs stakeholders at fifteen exactly as it does in points mode. Ranking drops magnitude, so the board shows each rep's underlying numbers next to their positions, and the top-to-median spread is read from those numbers, not from the ranks.

Each line has its own direction of better, and the agent ranks in that direction, so position one is always the best rep on that line and never just the biggest number. On most lines more is better. On the lower-is-better lines, cycle velocity and stall rate, the lowest value takes position one, since fewer days in stage and fewer stalled deals are the win. Multi-threading is higher-is-better, more engaged stakeholders per active deal is the stronger deal.

### Scoring when the data is imperfect

Score every line the data supports this week, at the fidelity it supports. A line that cannot be attributed across the whole team, a systemic data gap and not a rep with no activity, comes off the board that week: drop it, and the rest carry the board. In points mode renormalize the remaining weights to one hundred, in ranking mode leave it out of the weighted total. Name what was dropped and why, so the gap becomes the top of the fix list.

An event that cannot be verified is excluded from its line, never scored as zero and never ranked. Not verified means unknown, so a rep is never ranked down for a gap in the data. A real zero or a real last place counts only when the data exists and the rep simply did none of that action.

Stages are the team's own CRM stage values. Any customer action named on this page, a signed NDA or an opened sandbox, is an example of what a stage can mean, not a field the agent must hunt for. Read the stage from the CRM and credit the line when the opportunity reaches it.

Where a line can be read at more than one fidelity, name which one produced the score. Book a meeting from a meeting is the clearest case: a transcript showing the next step agreed in the room is the strong read, and a follow-up invite created the same day, tied to the same account and an external contact with duplicates collapsed, is the interim proxy, labeled a same-day follow-up and not a booking confirmed on the call. In-person meetings read the same way: a CRM in-person flag is the strong read, a calendar invite with a physical location and no video link is the labeled proxy. Score the fidelity the data supports, and say which it was.

### The weekly board: doing

The weights flex by segment, mid-market then enterprise, because in-person selling lives in the enterprise motion. Where in-person cannot be pulled, the line drops out and the rest carry the board.

| ID | Score | MM | ENT | Data source | Measure, last 7 days | Rule |
|----|-------|----|----|-------------|----------------------|------|
| W1 | New business meetings | 30 | 30 | Calendar and lead-source field, new-business accounts | Meetings held, split self-sourced vs inbound | min(1, effective/target), self-sourced 5x |
| W2 | New opportunities opened | 30 | 20 | Opportunity reaching your first qualified stage, read from the CRM stage value and not an artifact such as an NDA | Deals that reached that stage, split by source | min(1, effective/target), self-sourced 5x |
| W3 | In-person meetings | 5 | 15 | A CRM in-person flag is the strong read, a calendar invite with a physical location and no video link is the labeled proxy | In-person meetings on active deals | min(1, actual/target), drops out when no data |
| W4 | Book a meeting from a meeting | 20 | 20 | A booked next step tied to a held meeting, detected however the agent's access allows (call transcript, or a next invite that follows soon after) | Share of meetings held that led to a next meeting booked | min(1, rate/target) |
| W5 | Stakeholders added | 15 | 15 | Email and meeting activity on active deals | New engaged stakeholders on the deal, counted the way the team's data supports | min(1, actual/target) |

<Note>
  The self-sourced tag reads from the lead-source field, so the 5x rewards real hunting. The 5x applies only when source is known for a material share of the field: when most activity is unclassified, run W1 and W2 as raw counts and mark the source weighting pending, so the board ranks on who hunted and not on who tagged. In-person scores on active deals, so it rewards showing up to advance real deals and does not double-count a new-business meeting already in W1.
</Note>

### The monthly board: progress

Progress is conversion times velocity, and both read mid-cycle, so this board never waits on a close. Each line reads an event or a timestamp. The weights and windows flex by segment, because a long enterprise cycle must lean harder on late-stage progress and multi-threading, and read its rates over a longer window. Weights below show mid-market then enterprise.

| ID | Score | MM | ENT | Reads | Rule |
|----|-------|----|----|-------|------|
| M1 | NBM to opportunity conversion | 20 | 15 | Meetings held, then qualified-stage entry on the same account | min(1, rate/benchmark) |
| M2 | Opportunity to evaluation conversion | 15 | 15 | Qualified opps, then a live evaluation started, marked by the customer's first hands-on use | min(1, rate/benchmark) |
| M3 | Evaluation to late-stage conversion | 15 | 20 | Evaluations, then the buying committee engaged | min(1, rate/benchmark) |
| M4 | Cycle velocity | 20 | 15 | Days in stage, against a segment benchmark | min(1, benchmark/actual), lower days score higher |
| M5 | Stall rate | 15 | 15 | Active deals with no stage advance and no interaction in the window | min(1, benchmark/actual), lower stall scores higher |
| M6 | Multi-threading depth | 15 | 20 | Engaged stakeholders per active deal, a stock not the weekly flow | min(1, actual/benchmark) |

Conversion lines read over a trailing window so the denominators stay stable, roughly a quarter for mid-market and two to four quarters for enterprise. Velocity and stall read the current open portfolio against a segment benchmark, so a two-hundred-day enterprise deal is not flagged slow for being enterprise. Multi-threading depth runs on both segments, with a lower benchmark for mid-market, because getting wide across the buying group predicts a win, more so the larger the deal.

<Note>
  The per-step conversion is the coaching signal: it shows exactly where a rep's funnel breaks. Strong NBM-to-opp and weak opp-to-eval is a rep who gets meetings and qualifies well but cannot drive an evaluation, a named gap a full cycle before the quota miss. A rep with too few deals to form a stable rate has that line drop out and the rest carry, so a ramping rep is never ranked on noise.
</Note>

### The trailing result view

Quota and win rate are the only close-dependent measures, so they sit here, off the progress rank, read on a rolling window sized to the cycle. They are the lagging confirmation, and they are the check on progress that never converts.

| View | Reads | Window |
|------|-------|--------|
| Quota attainment, ramp-adjusted | Bookings vs a ramp-adjusted target | Quarter to date for mid-market, rolling four quarters for enterprise |
| Win rate | Closed-won over won plus lost | Trailing ninety days for mid-market, rolling twelve months for enterprise |

Read the monthly progress rank against this view. High progress with a weak trailing result is the happy-pipeline rep, advancing deals that do not close. Weak progress with a strong trailing result is a rep riding momentum who is about to slide. Both are talent signals you get early, on facts.

## When a signal is missing, work it out with the user

Every company runs its CRM, product analytics, and calendar on its own schema, so the fields and events named here will rarely match one to one, and only the team knows its roster and who is ramping. So before the first run the agent establishes the roster, the segments, and the ramp cohorts with the user, and resolves any missing, weak, or ambiguous signal with the user rather than stop. A line left unscored because no one was asked is a failure of the process, not a limit of the data.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the roster and cohorts">
    Before scoring, ask the user who is on the team, each rep's segment, and who is ramping with how long the ramp runs. The two tracks and the within-band ranking depend on it, and only the team knows it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look before concluding">
    Inspect what the systems actually hold before calling a line unscorable. Search for any field that could carry lead source, any product event that marks first use, any calendar or call record that shows a next step. The signal often exists under a different name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the gap, with its size">
    Tell the user which line is affected, what is missing, and what it blocks. Show the coverage, such as source known on three of seventeen meetings, so the size of the gap is clear and not a vague cannot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Offer alternatives, ranked by fidelity">
    Give two or three ways to close or approximate the gap, each with its tradeoff: the strong read, a weaker proxy, and exclusion. Recommend one, and never present a proxy as the strong read.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask one bounded question">
    Make the ask specific and answerable, usually one of the inputs in What each team supplies: where the activation event lives, which stage value marks a qualified deal, how a source should be credited. One decision at a time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the decision with a retire-by">
    Note what was agreed, label the affected line provisional, and name the data fix that retires the workaround. The run stays reproducible and the gaps become a closing list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Improve each week">
    Re-check whether a previously missing signal now exists, upgrade the line when it does, and tell the user the single data fix that would raise the score the most next week.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A refusal ends this loop. A question continues it, and the score starts provisional and gets more defensible every week, because the agent keeps asking for what it needs and the user keeps closing the gaps.

## How I run it every week

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pull the seven-day inputs">
    The five weekly scores come straight from the system of record over the last seven days. No one types a number, so the board is the same fact for everyone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rank, and read the spread">
    I stack-rank the team and watch the distance between the top rep and the median. Closing that gap is the point, because it means the playbook is transferring and the floor is coming up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coach to one change">
    In the one-to-one I name one thing the rep did well, one thing to do differently, and three commitments, anchored to the lines that move their number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read progress monthly">
    Once a month I rank the progress board and read it against the trailing result view, to see which rep's funnel is converting and moving, and whether progress is turning into closes. Movement in the rank is the early signal, so I watch who dropped before I watch who is on top.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Consistent and complementing

The three layers read the same funnel at increasing depth, so every weekly action has a monthly conversion it should produce, and the trailing view confirms whether it closed. Watching a step across the layers is the diagnosis.

| Weekly action, the doing | Monthly progress it should produce |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Meetings held | NBM to opportunity conversion |
| Opportunities opened | Opportunity to evaluation conversion |
| In-person meetings on active deals | Evaluation to late-stage conversion |
| Next steps booked | Cycle velocity, lower stall |
| Stakeholders added | Multi-threading depth |

<Note>
  When the weekly actions run green but the monthly conversion lags, the rep is doing the motions without advancing deals, a quality gap and not an effort gap. The trailing result view then confirms whether the progress is turning into closes. Each layer names the rep and the exact step.
</Note>

## Measure to tenure

A new hire and a tenured rep cannot sit in the same rank, because the new hire has not had time to build pipeline or reach a late stage. So reps run on two tracks, and the agent asks the user who is ramping and how long the ramp runs, because every team draws that line differently.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Ramp track" icon="seedling">
    Hire to ramp-complete. Scored on the doing board and the first conversions, judged against ramp milestones, and compared only within the ramping cohort. Developmental, and deliberately off the talent stack-rank.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Performance track" icon="award">
    Ramp-complete onward. The full three layers and the stack-rank, the talent read, compared within segment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The calculation adjusts as the rep ramps. A line they have not reached, a late-stage conversion or a close they have no deals for yet, is excluded for that rep and never scored zero, because it is not a miss. So the board starts as doing plus the first conversion and fills in as their deals move down the funnel, and you trust the doing board most early and the result most late. Rank within the ramp band so everyone compared has the same live lines, and when a band holds only a rep or two, fall back to the ramp-adjusted target or coach from the raw numbers without a rank.

## What the agent writes

For each rep the agent fills the weekly board and the monthly progress board, ranks the team on each within segment, and attaches the trailing result view as context. The enterprise profile and ranking mode are shown below. The headline I read first is the top-to-median spread and who moved.

Weekly board, ranking mode. Each cell is the rep's position on that line, the total is the weighted sum of positions, and the lowest total is rank one:

| Rep | W1 | W2 | W3 | W4 | W5 | Weighted total | Rank |
|-----|----|----|----|----|----|----------------|------|
| Rep A | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 115 | 1 |
| Rep C | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 200 | 2 |
| Rep B | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 285 | 3 |

Monthly progress board, ranking mode, with the trailing result alongside:

| Rep | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 | Weighted total | Rank | Move | Quota, trailing | Win rate, trailing |
|-----|----|----|----|----|----|----|----------------|------|------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Rep A | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 130 | 1 | +2 | 72% | 31% |
| Rep C | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 170 | 2 | 0 | 48% | 26% |
| Rep B | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 300 | 3 | -3 | 0% | n/a |

Example rows, showing the output shape. Positions are within segment, the lower total wins, and the Move column is the change in overall rank since last month, because the early signal is usually the slide. In points mode the same cells hold each line's points and the total becomes a composite out of one hundred. The agent also shows each rep's underlying numbers behind the positions, so the spread stays readable and no one reads a rank as a magnitude.

Alongside the scores, the agent reports the coverage behind each line, the share of events it could verify or classify, such as source known on W1 and W2, in-person coverage on W3, transcript and calendar coverage on W4, or the denominator behind each conversion line. A line scored on thin coverage is labeled provisional, so the board shows how firm each number is and no one reads a soft line as settled.

## What each team supplies

The method is complete on this page. In ranking mode it needs none of the targets, only the reps to compare. In points mode a few inputs make it run on your data.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The roster and ramp cohorts">Each rep's segment, and who is ramping with the ramp length, so the two tracks and the within-band ranking work. Only the team has this, so the agent asks.</Step>
  <Step title="Targets and benchmarks">Only for points mode: a value per line, per segment and ramp stage, weekly targets for the count lines and conversion and velocity benchmarks for the progress board. Ranking mode needs none of these.</Step>
  <Step title="A lead-source field">The source or first-touch tag that marks self-sourced from inbound, so the 5x is data and never a rep's own label.</Step>
  <Step title="A product activation event">A customer first-use signal piped to the data layer, which marks the evaluation conversion on the monthly board.</Step>
  <Step title="An in-person signal">A CRM in-person flag, or calendar location data to infer it, for the in-person line on the weekly board.</Step>
  <Step title="The CRM field names">The object, stage, and field names for the qualified stage, active-deal status, and contact roles, so the queries resolve.</Step>
  <Step title="Stage timestamps">Entry and exit times per stage. These drive the whole progress board: the conversion rates, the velocity, and the stall line all read them.</Step>
</Steps>

## What good looks like

- The weekly board is a fact, not a debate, because every line is a counted event.
- Every rep knows the one or two lines that move their number, and manages to them.
- The top-to-median spread is narrowing quarter over quarter.
- When the weekly actions run green but monthly progress lags, I know the gap is quality, not effort, and the board names the rep and the step.
- When progress runs green but the trailing result lags, I catch the happy-pipeline rep early, before the quarter confirms it.
