[Managing the inputs](/managing-inputs) tells a team what to watch. The scorecard is how I make the team run on it every week. It is two boards that read the same funnel at two depths: a weekly board for the inputs a rep controls, and a monthly board for what those inputs produce. I write the scoring out in full, so an agent can read this page, pull the CRM, and fill it in with nothing added but the team's own targets and field names.

<Note>
  Every line is a counted event. A meeting happened, a customer signed, a buyer logged in. That keeps the board a fact, and keeps every coaching conversation pointed at one.
</Note>

## Why it matters

Revenue is a lagging number, so I run the team on the inputs that produce it, and the scorecard is where those inputs become something I rank and coach on a Monday. The weekly board holds only what a rep controls right now, all of it counted events, so the ranking is fair and the one-to-one starts from a fact instead of an opinion. The monthly board holds the outcome those inputs produce, read on a slower clock where the data has settled. Run together, they show a rep their week and show me the whole engine.

## How I score it

Two rules cover every line. A count line scales to its target, and the two source-weighted lines value self-sourced work at five times inbound. I set a default weight on each line and tune it to the motion. The targets belong to the team, calibrated per segment and per ramp stage, so a new rep is measured against a ramp target and a tenured rep against a full one.

```
line_pct   = min(1, actual / target)        # count lines, when a target is set
line_pct   = actual / top_performer_actual  # provisional, when no target yet
effective  = 5 * self_sourced + 1 * inbound # source-weighted lines
line_pts   = line_pct * line_weight
composite  = sum(line_pts)                   # 0 to 100
```

The weekly board does not wait on calibration. When a line has an agreed target it scores against it. When it does not, the agent scores that line relative to the team that same week, indexing each rep to the current top performer, and a trailing team median works too, so the line still runs from zero to its weight. That keeps the stack-rank and the top-to-median spread live from the first Monday, which is the whole point of the weekly board. Mark those lines provisional, and swap in absolute targets as they are set. The monthly board always scores against absolute targets and benchmarks, because there the absolute number is the point.

### Scoring when the data is imperfect

Score every line the data supports this week, at the fidelity it supports. A line that cannot be attributed across the whole team, a systemic data gap and not a rep with no activity, comes off the board that week: drop it, renormalize the remaining weights to 100, and name what was dropped and why. A board on four honest lines still ranks the team, and the dropped line becomes the top of the fix list.

An event that cannot be verified is excluded from its line, never scored as zero. Not verified means unknown, so a rep is never ranked down for a gap in the data.

Stages are the team's own CRM stage values. Any customer action named on this page, a signed NDA or an opened sandbox, is an example of what a stage can mean, not a field the agent must hunt for. Read the stage from the CRM and credit the line when the opportunity reaches it.

Where a line can be read at more than one fidelity, name which one produced the score. Book a meeting from a meeting is the clearest case: a transcript showing the next step agreed in the room is the strong read, and a follow-up invite created the same day, tied to the same account and an external contact with duplicates collapsed, is the interim proxy, labeled a same-day follow-up and not a booking confirmed on the call. Score the fidelity the data supports, and say which it was.

### The weekly board

| ID | Score | Weight | Data source | Measure, last 7 days | Rule |
|----|-------|--------|-------------|----------------------|------|
| W1 | New business meetings | 30 | Calendar and lead-source field, new-business accounts | Meetings held, split self-sourced vs inbound | min(1, effective/target), self-sourced 5x |
| W2 | New opportunities opened | 25 | Opportunity reaching your first qualified stage, read from the CRM stage value and not an artifact such as an NDA | Deals that reached that stage, split by source | min(1, effective/target), self-sourced 5x |
| W3 | Customer activation | 20 | Product event, the customer's first hands-on use, a trial or sandbox login | Active deals with a customer login this week | min(1, actual/target) |
| W4 | Book a meeting from a meeting | 10 | A booked next step tied to a held meeting, detected however the agent's access allows (call transcript, or a next invite that follows soon after) | Share of meetings held that led to a next meeting booked | min(1, rate/target) |
| W5 | Stakeholders added | 15 | Email and meeting activity on active deals | New engaged stakeholders on the deal, counted the way the team's data supports | min(1, actual/target) |

<Note>
  The self-sourced tag reads from the lead-source field, so the 5x rewards real hunting. The 5x applies only when source is known for a material share of the field: when most activity is unclassified, run W1 and W2 as raw counts and mark the source weighting pending, so the board ranks on who hunted and not on who tagged. A stakeholder counts when there is real engagement, defined against the team's own data, so the line rewards multi-threading and not a bulk contact import.
</Note>

### The monthly board

| ID | Score | Weight | Data source | Rule |
|----|-------|--------|-------------|------|
| P1 | Quota attainment, ramp-adjusted | 35 | Bookings vs a ramp-adjusted target | graduated curve, and the top-tier gate |
| P2 | Win rate, evaluation to close | 25 | Closed-won over won plus lost, trailing ninety days | min(1, actual/benchmark) |
| P3 | Conversion health | 15 | Stage-to-stage rates across the funnel | composite vs benchmark |
| P4 | Late-stage velocity | 15 | Deals reaching the buying committee, paper started, days to close | blended vs target |
| P5 | Commercial quality | 10 | Contract terms, multi-year, durable commitments, retention | blended vs target |

<Note>
  Quota stays off the weekly board and anchors the monthly one. A rep reaches the top tier only once quota clears a ramp-adjusted floor, so the day-to-day stack-rank rewards the inputs a rep controls.
</Note>

## How I run it every week

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pull the seven-day inputs">
    The five weekly scores come straight from the system of record over the last seven days. No one types a number, so the board is the same fact for everyone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rank, and read the spread">
    I stack-rank the team and watch the distance between the top rep and the median. Closing that gap is the point, because it means the playbook is transferring and the floor is coming up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Coach to one change">
    In the one-to-one I name one thing the rep did well, one thing to do differently, and a single commitment, anchored to the line that moves their number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the outcomes monthly">
    Once a month I read the outcome board against the input-to-outcome map, to see which input is feeding, or starving, which result.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Consistent and complementing

The two boards are the same funnel at two depths, so every weekly input has a monthly outcome it feeds. Watching the pair is the diagnosis.

| Weekly input | The monthly outcome it moves |
|--------------|------------------------------|
| Meetings and opportunities created | Conversion health, then quota |
| Customer activation | Win rate, because an activated evaluation closes more often |
| Stakeholders added | Win rate and deals reaching the buying committee |
| Book a meeting from a meeting | Velocity, because a booked next step keeps the deal moving |

<Note>
  When the weekly inputs run green and the monthly outcomes lag, the work is conversion. When both run green, the engine is healthy and scaling. The weekly board already names the rep and the line.
</Note>

## What the agent writes

For each rep the agent fills this shape and ranks the team. The spread between the top rep and the median is the headline I read first.

| Rep | W1 | W2 | W3 | W4 | W5 | Weekly /100 | Rank | Monthly /100 |
|-----|----|----|----|----|----|-------------|------|--------------|
| Rep A | 27 | 25 | 20 | 10 | 12 | 94 | 1 | 71 |
| Rep B | 18 | 14 | 20 | 0 | 9 | 61 | 2 | 58 |
| Rep C | 12 | 10 | 8 | 10 | 6 | 46 | 3 | 44 |

Example rows, showing the output shape. The agent replaces them with live data.

Alongside the scores, the agent reports the coverage behind each line, the share of events it could verify or classify, such as source known on W1 and W2 or transcript and calendar coverage on W4. A line scored on thin coverage is labeled provisional, so the board shows how firm each number is and no one reads a soft line as settled.

## What each team supplies

The method is complete on this page. Two inputs make it run on your data, and three more unlock the parked lines.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Targets">A value per line, per segment and ramp stage. The monthly board needs them. The weekly board uses them when set and scores relative to the team when they are not, so it runs without waiting.</Step>
  <Step title="A lead-source field">The source or first-touch tag that marks self-sourced from inbound, so the 5x is data and never a rep's own label.</Step>
  <Step title="A product activation event">A customer-login signal piped to the data layer, for the activation line.</Step>
  <Step title="The CRM field names">The object, stage, and field names for the qualified stage, active-deal status, and contact roles, so the queries resolve.</Step>
  <Step title="Stage timestamps">Entry and exit times per stage, which unlock stall control on the weekly board and velocity on the monthly.</Step>
</Steps>

## What good looks like

- The weekly board is a fact, not a debate, because every line is a counted event.
- Every rep knows the one or two lines that move their number, and manages to them.
- The top-to-median spread is narrowing quarter over quarter.
- When the monthly outcomes lag a green weekly board, I know the work is conversion, and the board already names the rep and the line.
