A hunting team is built one hire at a time. I hire to a clear profile, run the funnel like an outbound motion so it stays full of people who already fit, and put every finalist through a live sell so I see the real selling before I make an offer. This is the profile I hire to and the funnel that finds it.

## Why it matters

The hire is the most leveraged decision a sales leader makes, because it compounds. A strong rep lifts the people around them, sets the pace the team ramps to, and makes the next candidate easier to attract. Running a high-bar funnel at real volume is how each new seat raises the whole team, one hire at a time.

<Note>
  Hire for the motion and the character. The product is teachable. The drive and the way someone handles a live sell are what you are really hiring.
</Note>

## How I build it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hire to a clear profile">
    The strongest signal is a rep who climbed from SDR to AE to senior AE at the same company, because it shows they earned each promotion where people knew their work. I give real weight to front-line backgrounds, retail, hospitality, teaching, which correlate with top performance, because those people learned to win a room over every day. Character and drive lead; the product knowledge comes with ramp.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Source like an outbound motion: the 1-2 punch">
    I run sourcing the way I run pipeline generation. A recruiter opens with a personalized reach-out, and I follow up myself a couple of days later, run as a deliberate cadence. I mine LinkedIn for quota-attainment and top-performer signals, so the funnel stays full of people who already carry the profile and feel personally pursued by the hiring leader.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Screen with a live sell">
    The second screen is a role-play where the candidate sells what they sell today, in their own words, and then takes live feedback in the room. The sell shows me the motion they actually run. How they take the feedback shows me how they will grow. That one exercise tells me more than a resume ever could.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hire in pairs, and size to capacity">
    I add seats in pairs, so new hires ramp alongside a peer and set a pace for each other. I open a seat when the deal load has outgrown the team and the pipeline already supports it, so every hire lands on real work from day one. A mid-market rep carries roughly 20 to 25 active deals at healthy capacity, about half that in enterprise, and I size the team to that math.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Character over credential

<Tip>
  Some of the strongest reps I have hired came from outside software. One I brought in from teaching became a top performer and still calls me years later for advice. The through-line is consistent: people who spent years earning attention and handling a hard moment in person tend to carry that straight into selling. I hire for that instinct and teach the product on top of it.
</Tip>

## What good looks like

- Every new seat raises the team's bar, because each hire clears the same high profile.
- The live-sell screen predicts ramp: the reps who take feedback well in the room are the ones who ramp fastest on the floor.
- Climbed-the-ladder and front-line backgrounds show up heavily among the top performers.
- Sourcing runs like a cadence, so there is always a warm bench of profile-fit candidates to pull from.
